# Lesson 01 Research Brief — Arrival in Copenhagen

## Product premise
Platå Lesson 01 should not feel like a grammar page. It is a short interactive episode: the learner has just arrived in Copenhagen and Danish is the interface to the world.

## Pedagogical scope
Target: absolute beginner / early A1. Keep cognitive load small.

Learning outcomes:
- greet someone: `Hej!`
- introduce yourself: `Jeg hedder ...`
- answer `Hvad hedder du?`
- understand `tak`, `selv tak`, `undskyld`
- recognise `indgang` / `udgang`
- complete one tiny social exchange without switching to English

Avoid in Lesson 01:
- full verb conjugation paradigms
- noun gender theory
- V2 terminology
- subordinate clauses
- audio-only gates that block progress

## Micro language set
Active:
- `Hej` — hi / bye
- `Jeg hedder ...` — my name is ...
- `Hvad hedder du?` — what is your name?
- `Tak` — thanks
- `Selv tak` — you're welcome
- `Undskyld` — excuse me
- `Ja` / `Nej` — yes / no

Passive / recognition:
- `Velkommen til København`
- `Indgang`
- `Udgang`
- `Næste station`
- `Reservation`
- `Værelse`

## Learner traps
- Writing `Jeg hedder mig ...` by analogy with other languages.
- Treating `hej` only as English “hi”, missing goodbye use.
- Confusing `indgang` and `udgang`.
- Forgetting `tak` as a social lubricant.
- Overloading the first lesson with grammar before the learner has any wins.

## Narrative learning UX principles
- Exercise-as-action: every task is something the character does in the scene.
- Micro-tension → micro-payoff: small uncertainty, immediate response.
- Errors are story feedback, not red failure banners.
- Repetition through place: a word appears in signage, dialogue, and final recall.
- Diegetic UI: choices appear as signs, receipts, phone messages, forms.

## Proposed episode arc
1. Arrival: read the `Velkommen` sign and find `udgang`.
2. Meet Lene: answer `Hvad hedder du?`.
3. Door/sign task: distinguish `indgang` / `udgang`.
4. Tiny courtesy exchange: `tak` / `selv tak`.
5. Final handoff: say hello to a roommate and complete the first Danish sentence.

## Success criterion
The learner should leave with one feeling: “I just handled a tiny real situation in Danish.”
