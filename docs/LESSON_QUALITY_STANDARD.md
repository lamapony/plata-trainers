# Platå lesson quality standard

The lesson engine proves that a path is playable. This standard defines whether the lesson is worth playing.

## The learner promise

Every lesson rehearses one situation an adult learner may actually face in Denmark. By the end, the learner should be able to say or write something useful in a similar situation without copying the original answer word for word.

## Required shape

1. **Concrete opening.** Name the place, relationship, goal, and pressure in plain language.
2. **Usable Danish.** Teach complete phrases that a person could say or send today.
3. **Plausible choices.** Wrong answers must be believable near misses. Do not use joke answers, panic exits, or cartoon villains.
4. **Observable feedback.** Explain what the listener can and cannot understand, then give one repair the learner can reuse.
5. **Increasing independence.** Move from recognition to production. The final task must use the skill in a new channel, person, or situation.
6. **Source discipline.** Rules about public services, housing, health, or other real-world processes need a current primary source. The lesson practises language; it does not replace professional advice.

## Voice and language

- Interface explanations use clear English. Danish appears in dialogue, examples, and answers.
- Do not mix English and Danish grammar inside one explanatory sentence.
- Keep internal learning-design terms out of learner copy: `Diagnostic`, `Carry-forward`, `Unlocked`, `agency`, `low-signal`, `memory-backed`, and `channel transfer`.
- Prefer a concrete consequence over a judgement. Write “the landlord still has no date to confirm”, not “this is a weak social signal”.
- A grammatical alternative may be wrong for the requested task, but feedback must explicitly say that the sentence itself is grammatical.

## Review gates

All reviewed lessons declare `contentVersion: 2`. CI checks schema, simulation paths, completion answers, editorial phrase rules, learner-visible jargon, duplicate options, source references, and the flagship exercise contract.

An editor must still read the complete learner path on a phone-sized screen. Automated checks cannot decide whether a dialogue sounds natural, a distractor is respectful, or a final task represents useful transfer.
